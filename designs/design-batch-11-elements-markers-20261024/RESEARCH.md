# Research: four elements and generic sky markers

## Elemental characters

The final [Unicode 17 NamesList](https://www.unicode.org/Public/17.0.0/ucd/NamesList.txt) assigns U+1F701–U+1F704 to Air, Fire, Earth and Water in the alchemical block. The [Indiana University alchemical-symbol proposal](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2009/09037-alchemy.pdf), Table 2 on PDF page 12, shows the upright/downward triangle family and the Air/Earth crossbars. The university's [Chymistry of Isaac Newton symbol guide](https://newton.dlib.indiana.edu/page/symbols) provides an additional scholarly reference to those elemental forms.

**Design interpretation:** retain the existing own Trine triangle as the common motif. Fire deliberately shares that geometry. Add a full-width middle bar for Air; reflect Fire and Air vertically to obtain Water and Earth. These are different character identities even where basic geometry is shared. The physical Earth icon is a separate existing drawing. No planetary, aspect or elemental calculation is selected by an outline or style feature.

## Three generic markers

The final UCD identifies U+2605 as BLACK STAR and U+2604 as COMET. The pinned Unicode 17 miscellaneous-symbol chart illustrates their structures. The fixed-star use of BLACK STAR is the inventory's contextual chart convention, not a dedicated codepoint for every named star.

U+2042 ASTERISM appears in the General Punctuation block; the pinned [Unicode 17 chart](https://www.unicode.org/charts/PDF/Unicode-17.0/U170-2000.pdf) illustrates a group of three asterisks. The inventory adopts that shape for a generic chart annotation. This does not establish a universal astronomical emblem system for constellations or named asterisms, and it does not change the character's normal typographic role.

**Design interpretation:** a broad five-point filled star; an open circular comet head with a clear three-stroke fan; and three separated six-ray asterisk clusters. Their geometry is authored from simple points, paths and circles. The comet fan is schematic and carries no physical tail-vector or tail-count claim. Specific star, comet and pattern identities remain names and structured chart data.

## Text presentation and mapping

The frozen [Unicode 17 variation data](https://www.unicode.org/Public/17.0.0/ucd/emoji/emoji-variation-sequences.txt) register U+2604 FE0E for Comet text presentation. A later font build should support and check that sequence alongside the ordinary character. The other six studies do not receive invented presentation sequences. No new PUA, ssXX assignment or automatic word replacement is needed for this design packet.

The final mappings remain the frozen Unicode 17 assignments. The 2009 proposal's temporary XX positions are historical development evidence, not current character numbers. Unversioned live chart URLs can advance; this packet uses pinned or frozen version-17 data for identity.

## Comparison choices

The preserved controls are Trine, physical Earth, Sextile, Varda, Juno and Batch 10 Occultation. Fire/Trine intentionally match. The other sheets examine useful differences: barred downward triangle versus circled cross, solid five-point star versus existing ray/cross constructions, open three-ray comet versus solid single-stem event mark, and a three-part punctuation group versus one six-rayed aspect.

All small-size examples are source-vector studies. They are not engine results, revised native model definitions or actual-font shaping proofs. No external font outline, PDF glyph path or source image is extracted or traced into a master.
