# Symbol design decisions

The user requested actual symbols and authorized invention where necessary. The previous Ta/Hx/Sn/Ut composition direction is superseded. Full names remain ordinary text, while each new mark is a path-only symbol.

| Symbol | Construction | Design rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Tao | Two opposed open hooks | An opposed pair with a central channel; a separate silhouette from opposition, the comet and native Qd. |
| Hexadecile | Nested open angles | Repeating the semisquare-like turn suggests halving 45°. The inner gap was widened after the first 24 px raster check. |
| Seminovile | Cleft diamond with a separate dividing stroke | A divided figure suggests half a novile. The lower opening and interior stroke distinguish it from a closed diamond. |
| Untion | Open fork with two separate horizontal levels | A second division bar extends the visual grammar of the 30° semisextile toward 15°. |

These are new visual mnemonics, not claims about astrological effects, historical doctrine or exact protractor angles in the drawings.

All four use a 1,000-unit square cell and 64-unit round-ended strokes. They retain open gaps and simple silhouettes, with no miniature letters, numbers or hidden font substitutions. Source paths are editable SVG masters. Each new record explicitly marks its origin as `moira_original_invented_symbol` and remains pending owner review.

The comparison bank preserves 189 source records: 178 earlier controls, four existing Batch 01 aspects and seven Batch 11 element/sky markers. This is a record count, not a unique character count. Their outlines and advances are not modified.

The current 0.10.0 TTF/WOFF2 files remain intact. New symbols have no Unicode, private-use, token, feature or variation assignment. No model, native aspect admission, orb or applying/separating policy is changed. A later font-integration step can allocate explicit access after design approval, retaining the native 24° Quindecile route.

At 14 px em, retain a legend and inspect the intended chart density. The proof sheets provide rendering evidence; they do not establish universal recognition or uniqueness against every symbol ever used.
