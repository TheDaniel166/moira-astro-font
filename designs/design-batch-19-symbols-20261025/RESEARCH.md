# Research and provenance

25 October 2026. A published symbol witness is distinct from a standard, and a new Moira form is distinct from either. These four drawings are original inventions; none is presented as an exact historical reproduction.

## Tao at 165°

Tao remains the user-selected primary name. The authored Planet Figures discussion also uses tao for the 165° side of a Telescope configuration. [Krap, Planet Figures](https://astrologyplanetfigures.wordpress.com/) · [retained HTML](references/krap-planet-figures.html).

**A published nonletter 165° form does exist.** Halloran's own software notes explicitly describe the symbol he created: a modified opposition with a lower ring and two spreading rays. The linked GIF was retrieved and visually inspected. Its resemblance to a comet is acknowledged by the source. [Halloran, Customizable Quindecile / Font Mapper](https://www.halloran.com/dlr5feat.htm) · [HTML](references/halloran-v5.html) · [source GIF](references/halloran-QDC.gif).

That forked-circle form is retained as a source witness. The existing Moira comet has a ring and a ray fan, so the proposed Tao uses different geometry: two open opposed hooks. **This is an original Moira invention**, not a rendering of Halloran's symbol and not an attested historical Tao sigil. Future compatibility variants can be considered explicitly.

### Correction to the earlier packet

The earlier Batch 19 notes incorrectly identified the hooked shape in Cochrane's p5 table as a 165° glyph. Reinspection shows that it belongs to the **135° Sesquiquadrate row**; the 165° row has no separate drawn glyph. Glyph and text columns have slightly different vertical spacing. Cochrane remains evidence for **165° and 11/24**, not for this Tao design. The old packet and its source-observation file remain unchanged; this packet records the correction. [Cochrane PDF](https://www.astrosoftware.com/Pub/Tech/FeisBlog/BasicAlphabetOfAstrology.pdf) · [local PDF](references/fei-basic-alphabet.pdf) · [inspected p5](references/fei-p5.png).

## Hexadecile, Seminovile and Untion

The inspected primary sources establish terminology, fractions or software usage. They do **not** establish a unique nonletter symbol for these three concepts. This is a limit of the inspected evidence, not a claim that no symbol exists anywhere.

Suignard's Table 1 lists Seminovile at 1/18 and Untion at 1/24 in the astrology column. Hexadecile at 1/16 appears in the statistics column, with the astrology cell blank. The old Batch 02 claim about that particular witness is not carried forward. [N4811, physical p2](https://www.unicode.org/wg2/docs/n4811-fdbk-extra-aspect-sym.pdf) · [local PDF](references/suignard-n4811.pdf) · [table image](references/suignard-p2.png).

Wharton's printed p92 supplies historical astrological use of the word Hexadecile, but no explicit 22.5° value or distinct symbol in that passage. [Original page](https://books.google.com/books?id=LBroWVBBY2oC&pg=PA92) · [retained page image](references/wharton1683-p92-image).

Astro Gold uses ordinary letters for several minor aspects, including these angles under varying names. Aureas independently lists 22.5° Semioctile, 20° Seminovile and 15° Quartosextile. Those sources support angle/alias usage; letters are not the chosen design direction in this packet. [Astro Gold manual](https://www.astrogold.io/AG-MacOS-Help/aspects.html), [Aureas notes](https://www.aureas-astrology.com/contents/en-us/d1_News.html).

The new nested-angle, cleft-diamond and double-bar-fork shapes are Moira inventions. Their geometric mnemonics explain design decisions and carry no historical or interpretive authority.
