## Lunar definitions and their limits

Mean and osculating perigee can be opposite their corresponding apogee when both come from the same selected apsidal model or ellipse. “Interpolated” methods require their own definition; their apogee/perigee outputs must not simply be assumed to form a fixed 180° pair. Astrodienst documents one natural/interpolated implementation and its method history. It is a convention witness, not a command to adopt its calculations. [Own documentation, sections 2.2.1–2.2.4](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm).

Faulks documents a Priapus outlined-crescent family and multiple Black/White Moon forms. His samples show that source programs attach different meanings to similar appearances. Proserpina has dot/ring variants; plain and rayed White Moon marks coexist; the Arta cradle is also documented. The original proposal's suggested allocations are superseded. [Faulks, L2/16-080, printed pp6, 22–23, 29–31](https://www.unicode.org/L2/L2016/16080-add-astrology.pdf).

Our mean bar, osculating diamond and interpolation bridge are **Moira-original discriminators**, not historical universal model symbols. Each marks a separate semantic identity; the actual lunar model and interpolation method remain metadata and readable text.

## Selena and Arta

Timashev's own history distinguishes earlier empirical fixed-cycle White Moon tables from his later construction. His main article describes an authored relationship involving the lunar node, Dark Moon and solar apogee, and explicitly discusses a quarter-circle/branch ambiguity. This supports a **Timashev-qualified Arta** label, not a uniquely specified implementation from the name alone. The paper's physical and interpretive claims are not adopted by this font. [Original author article](https://astrologer.ru/book/light_moon/index.html.en), [author history](https://astrologer.ru/book/light_moon/history.html.en).

Selena therefore stays convention-qualified. It is not automatically lunar perigee, a simple opposite of Lilith or a synonym of Arta. Faulks reports that some ZET configurations use rayed/plain appearances to distinguish different White Moon conventions. The proposed three-ray drawing is an original adaptation of that source family; selecting it does not select a calculation.

## Specialist forms

Jan Kampherbeek's own manual places several Vulcan traditions side by side, including a forked staff/cross and a downward-arrow alternative labeled Vulcan Leverrier. Its list separates Hamburg Vulkanus and Carteret Vulcan. We redraw the two intramercurial form witnesses without copying font contours or choosing orbital elements. [Enigma author manual, physical p9](https://radixpro.com/dl/EnigmaAstrology2_en.pdf).

The software-author documentation also distinguishes intramercurial Vulcan, White Moon Selena and Waldemath's hypothetical second Moon. It records competing Waldemath element interpretations. No unique nonletter Waldemath primary form was verified in the sources inspected here, so the disk/interrupted-orbit emblem is explicitly invented. The original 1898 report was not retrieved; we make no claim to reproduce its notation. [Astrodienst, sections 2.7.5–2.7.7](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swisseph.htm), [Astrolog author note on Vulcan](https://www.astrolog.org/ftp/updat600.htm).

Ophiuchus has an assigned Unicode symbol and a recognizable reference family. Its use here requires a declared modern zodiac convention. This does not alter Moira's twelve-sign model or select astronomical constellation boundaries. NASA's current constellation page supplies terminology only; its former starfinder URL now redirects and was not used to establish a particular thirteen-sign scheme. [Unicode symbol chart](https://www.unicode.org/charts/PDF/U2600.pdf), [NASA constellation terminology](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/constellations/en/).
